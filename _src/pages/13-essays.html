Essays in a borrowed voice

Occasional essays on law, theology and the state. Each is written in the register of a writer I admire, and says so. The voice is borrowed; the argument, and its errors, are mine. There are also some biblical reflections, in the same spirit.

  • In the register of Søren Kierkegaard · October 2026

    The Patient Who Cannot Be Guilty

    The confessional replaced by the clinic, not by denial but by translation: soul into ego, sin into trauma, absolution into self-acceptance. Why guilt is the dignity of a spirit and not a symptom, why the boundary is the death of the neighbour, why the man who works on himself for ten years never begins, and the one formula by which despair is removed.

  • In the register of St Jerome · October 2026

    Half of Nothing

    Not Caesar but Herod Antipas: the tetrarch who built his capital on a graveyard, took his brother’s wife, feared one man in camel hair, and had him beheaded in the middle of a party rather than be seen taking back a boast. The pattern of the petty ruler, the nation that prefers the fox in the palace to the voice in the wasteland, and the reckoning that follows.

On remote work and the empty forum: why a vote was once a walk across a floor, why courage needs a witness as fire needs air, and why a tyrant has no need of guards when every citizen has placed himself under house arrest.
