In a child’s words. Before you decide what a sentence means, list everything it could mean, and keep each meaning until something rules it out.

The Institute habit

Every lesson at the Institute carried a second message under the first, and the students were drilled until they no longer noticed it.

The book

From the newspaper advertisement through the tests to Mr. Benedict’s first explanation. Reynie noticed that the advertisement spoke to children, not to their parents. Ask what else it leaves unsaid.

Week 1 · the kind of answer

Monday: sentences with two readings (“Visiting relatives can be boring”; “I saw the girl with the telescope”). For each, follow both readings to their end: what else would have to be true? Wednesday: read the advertisement two ways, as an honest offer and as a trap, and say what each reading predicts you would find at the test.

Week 2 · into the gap

Reading is still a gap on the map. The child writes a 300-word essay for it, as a letter from Reynie to Miss Perumal, about a sentence they once misread.

Week 3 · the case

Joshua 10:12–13, “the sun stood still”, can be read as a description of how things looked, or as astronomy. Read as of 1700, before the question was settled, both readings stay open: interpretation alone was not allowed to decide it. Leave it open. Unit 3 comes back to it.
