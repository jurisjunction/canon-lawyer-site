Reading and Number: Art of Problem Solving

AoPS books open every section with problems before any explanation. Richard Rusczyk’s phrase for it is “We show the problems first.” That is Mr. Benedict’s Monday puzzle, worked before anything is explained, so the Monday puzzle can be the opening problems of the week’s section.

A caution about Alcumus. AoPS’s practice site picks the next problem by a running measure of the child and keeps a level that can drop with a miss. For a child who reads that as a verdict on himself, leave it out: the book’s problems and the class’s challenge problems are enough. Measuring is fine. Letting the measure choose the next problem puts it ahead of the order, and the Number unit’s own rule is that order comes first and measure is laid on it.

Latin

Ørberg’s Familia Romana is written so that the meaning of each new word comes from the Latin around it and the pictures in the margin, before the grammar is named. It is the Reading unit’s rule from the inside: every sentence is read before it is explained. Later in the year the Latin comes back on its own: the Vulgate in Unit 8, the Code of Canon Law (written in Latin) in Unit 11, and the Ordinary of the Mass in Unit 12.

Big History and photography

Big History, from the Big History Project (free from the OER Project, or in homeschool versions such as Rise Out’s), runs through eight thresholds of increasing complexity, from the Big Bang to the modern revolution. It carries Nature and Memory; see Science from final causes for how to keep it in its kind.

Photography is Making with Number inside it. Each full stop of aperture or shutter halves or doubles the light: shutter speeds run 1/125, 1/250, 1/500, and f-numbers step by √2. The marked numbers are rounded measures laid on the doubling. 1/125 stands for 1/128, and f/11 for about 11.31: Unit 2’s rule, inside a camera.
