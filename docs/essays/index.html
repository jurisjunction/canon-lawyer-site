Essays in a borrowed voice

Occasional essays on law, theology and the state. Each is written in the register of a writer I admire, and says so. The voice is borrowed; the argument, and its errors, are mine. There are also some biblical reflections, in the same spirit.

  • In the register of G. K. Chesterton · October 2026

    The Rented Hour

    There is a small glowing oblong in every pocket, and inside it is a grid of twenty-minute rectangles, given away to strangers. What the digital calendar has done to the season, the promise and the feast, and to the liberty, friendship and fallow ground of the man who owns it; and the one white rectangle worth defending.

    Read the essay →

  • In the register of Abraham Joshua Heschel · October 2026

    A Plate of Ice

    A grandmother of thirty-three years in California and a stateless father in Ohio, both taken at routine check-ins. What the Law of Moses says about the stranger who has begotten children among you, why the Church’s own law has no category for an illegal person, and what a penalty without a crime is called.

    Read the essay →

  • In the register of Herbert McCabe · October 2026

    On a People That Made Itself

    “We the People … do ordain and establish.” A sentence spoken by a thing that does not yet exist until it is spoken. What a state is, the three answers to where authority comes from, the two American readings, what the jurists admit, and the narrow thing that actually follows.

    Read the essay →
