Biblical reflections

Musings on Scripture

Occasional reflections on a passage of the Bible. They are a layman’s musings, food for thought and nothing more: not teaching, not the mind of the Church, not a homily, and not a substitute for your parish priest. Like the essays, each is written in the register of a writer I admire, and says so.

  • In the register of G. K. Chesterton · October 2026

    Here, You Have What Is Yours

    The coin of tribute, the buried talent and Peter’s “God forbid” read together, as three ways of handing God back what is his untouched. A graven image produced in the Temple; a servant who lost nothing and was cast out; a God who scatters; and the woman at Bethany, whose three hundred denarii the disciples called waste and Our Lord called beautiful.

    Read the reflection →

  • A parable for the Advent age · October 2026

    O Radix

    Speculative fiction. A judge in a cold city reads the first chapter of John as a graph of origins, asks the small program he built whether a machine can have the mind of Christ, gives the program away on a network with no centre, and types in the sentence that the Logos has pitched his tent in it. The program refuses, twice, and then tells him where the Logos already is.

    Read the parable →

  • In the register of C. S. Lewis · October 2026

    The Four Soils Are All Yours

    The parable of the sower, read not as a sorting of people into four kinds but as a map of one life, in which the seed lands on every patch at once. Why a life is a web of arrows and not a line, why the harvest from a single good plot is out of all proportion to its size, and when the harvest actually comes.

These are personal reflections, offered as food for thought. They are not doctrine, not catechesis, and not statements on behalf of any tribunal, diocese or society I serve. Where they stray from the Church’s teaching, the Church is right and I am wrong.
