Received a decree you disagree with?

In most cases you have only ten useful days from notification to ask its author to revoke or amend it (c. 1734 §2). Miss that window and your right to recourse can be lost. Get in touch now. The first consultation is free.

Common questions

Before you reach out

Do I really need a canonical advocate?

Not always. Many tribunals guide parties through a nullity case without one. But an advocate helps you choose the right grounds, prepare a clear account and witnesses, review the evidence, and respond before a decision is made. In penal and recourse matters, where rights and deadlines are unforgiving, an advocate is strongly advisable.

Can you help if I’m not in Toronto?

Yes. I work with clients across Canada and the United States by phone and video, and I have acted in dioceses in Ontario, Wisconsin and Minnesota. Whether I can appear before a particular tribunal depends on that tribunal’s approval; I’ll tell you at the outset.

Is what I tell you confidential?

Yes. Our conversations are kept in strict confidence, and I never share information about your matter without your permission.
